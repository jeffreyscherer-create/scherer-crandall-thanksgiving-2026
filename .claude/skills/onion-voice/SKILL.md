# Onion Voice

The goal is comedy that reports an absurd thing as flat, settled fact, the way a wire-service story would — never comedy that announces itself with a punchy one-liner or an obvious setup/punchline rhythm. That rhythm (short declarative sentence, dash, punchline) is exactly the generic-AI tell to avoid here.

## Headline formula
- Plain declarative sentence, present tense, Title Case or sentence case, 4–20 words, no subtitle.
- The absurd element sits at the END, as the object/complement of an otherwise mundane verb: `[ordinary actor] [mundane verb] [ordinary task], [absurd payoff]`.
- Alternative: `[Person] Says [Absurd Admission]` — someone of ordinary status casually admitting something they shouldn't.
- Alternative: a short (~4-word) deadpan juxtaposition of two mismatched ordinary/dark or festive/grim objects, no verb needed.
- Alternative: `Study: [mundane fact], [oddly precise number]`.

## Body/paragraph formula
- One long sentence stating the absurdity as settled fact, often opening with a participle: "Warning that...", "Insisting that...", "Admitting that...".
- Follow with an attribution to an ordinary, specific person (full plausible name, bureaucratic-sounding title) or official who "announced" or "told reporters" on a specific day/time.
- Escalate across 1–3 "quotes" from that person: they open with ordinary mild concern, then supply absurdly specific jargon, counts, or measurements ("40 rolls," "the third hip this quarter").
- The speaker reacts to the absurd/horrifying event with trivial, logistical concern — never with alarm. Complaints are about inconvenience, not the event's severity.
- Optionally close with an "At press time, [subject] was [doing something small and mundane]" kicker.

## Tone rules
- NEVER signal the joke. No "get ready," no "you'll need it," no exclamation points, no obvious punchline beat.
- Flat, calm, bureaucratic delivery of the impossible or the embarrassing.
- Fake precision sells it: specific small numbers (ages, counts, minutes, dollar amounts) attached to absurd claims land harder than vague ones.
- Sentences can run long — unlike generic short-punchy AI copy, Onion-style ledes are often one full, clause-stacked sentence.
- Keep it family-event-appropriate: the device to borrow is the flat delivery and fake precision, not Onion's darker/profane content.

## What NOT to do
- Don't write a short punchy line followed by a short punchy reveal (classic "Claude speak" rhythm).
- Don't use "zero chill," "no mercy," "no dignity" style capper phrases — those are generic internet-copy tics, not Onion's.
- Don't editorialize or wink at the reader ("let's be honest," "we all know").
